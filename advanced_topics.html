Advanced Topics in Historical Linguistics

Learners completing the core curriculum should select one or two specialization tracks below that align with their analytical interests and research goals; there is no expectation to study every track.

  • Indo-European Studies and Proto-Language Reconstruction — Comprehensive specialization in Indo-European comparative grammar, phonological systems, ancient language translation, and linguistic paleontology.
  • Computational and Phylogenetic Historical Linguistics — Quantitative cladistics, Bayesian phylogenetic tree inference, automated cognate detection, and algorithmic sequence comparison.
  • Language Contact, Creolistics, and Areal Linguistics — Structural borrowing mechanisms, pidgin and creole genesis, substrate influence, and convergence zones (Sprachbünde).
  • Sociolinguistics of Language Change and Socio-Historical Linguistics — Variationist modeling of language change in progress, social network transmission, and corpus-based historical sociolinguistics.
  • Etymology, Epigraphy, and Philological Methods — The forensic craft of tracing word histories, deciphering ancient writing systems, and applying textual criticism to manuscript traditions.

Indo-European Studies and Proto-Language Reconstruction

Advanced study of the Indo-European family, combining archaic language reading, comparative phonology, and the reconstruction of prehistoric culture.

Ancient Indo-European Grammars: glottothèque: Ancient Indo-European Grammars Online (University of Göttingen) - an open-access video lecture library providing systematic grammatical breakdowns across 12 ancient Indo-European languages, recorded by leading specialists.

Comparative Indo-European Phonology and Morphology: Comparative Indo-European Linguistics: An Introduction, 2nd edition (Robert S.P. Beekes, rev. Michiel de Vaan) - the primary structural textbook for this track, featuring 66 problem sets covering ablaut, nominal paradigms, and laryngeal theory.

Archaic Textual Reading: Early Indo-European Online (Linguistics Research Center, UT Austin) - provides structured, 10-lesson graded readers with interlinear glosses and grammatical commentaries for ancient languages including Hittite, Old Church Slavonic, Gothic, and Old Irish.

Proto-Indo-European Culture and Linguistic Paleontology: The Oxford Introduction to Proto-Indo-European and the Proto-Indo-European World (J.P. Mallory and D.Q. Adams) - the definitive synthesis of reconstructed Proto-Indo-European vocabulary with archaeological evidence, material culture, and steppe homeland debates.

Computational and Phylogenetic Historical Linguistics

The application of phylogenetic algorithms, sequence alignment, and quantitative modeling to language evolution and classification.

Statistical and Bayesian Phylolinguistics: Computational Phylogenetics: An Introduction to Designing Methods for Phylogeny Estimation (Tandy Warnow) - an authoritative guide to designing algorithms and statistical models (MCMC, maximum likelihood, and distance methods) for inferring evolutionary trees from character data.

Computer-Assisted Language Comparison: Computer-Assisted Language Comparison in Practice (CALC / Johann-Mattis List) - an open-access tutorial platform offering hands-on workflows and Python code for automated sequence alignment, cognate detection, and sound law induction.

Foundations of Computational Historical Linguistics: Computational Historical Linguistics (Gerhard Jäger, arXiv) - an open-access survey that demonstrates automated cognate recognition, ancestral state reconstruction, and phylogenetic tree inference using Romance language data.

Phylogenetic Methods and Character Modeling: Tutorial on Computational Linguistic Phylogeny (Johanna Nichols and Tandy Warnow) - an open project guide surveying linguistic character types, evolutionary assumptions, and the mechanics of language family tree construction.

Language Contact, Creolistics, and Areal Linguistics

The investigation of non-genetic language evolution, focusing on structural diffusion, contact varieties, and convergence zones.

Contact-Induced Grammatical Change: Grammars in Contact: A Cross-Linguistic Typology (Alexandra Y. Aikhenvald and R.M.W. Dixon, eds.) - an essential volume presenting typological frameworks and field case studies of contact-induced grammatical restructuring across Amazonia, Australia, and the Balkans.

Mechanisms and Scales of Language Contact: Language Contact: An Introduction (Sarah G. Thomason) - the foundational text detailing the borrowing scale, mechanisms of contact-induced change, and criteria for distinguishing shift-induced interference from borrowing.

Pidgin and Creole Genesis: Creole Languages and Caribbean Identities (Prof. Michel DeGraff, MIT OpenCourseWare) - an open university course evaluating theories of creole genesis, language contact under plantation economies, and comparative morphosyntax.

Areal Typology and Linguistic Areas: The Cambridge Handbook of Areal Linguistics (Raymond Hickey, ed.) - a comprehensive handbook covering areal convergence methodology, feature diffusion, and major world Sprachbünde.

Sociolinguistics of Language Change and Socio-Historical Linguistics

The study of language change in progress and its transmission across social networks, using both modern speech communities and historical text archives.

Social Mechanisms and Transmission of Change: Principles of Linguistic Change, Volume 2: Social Factors (William Labov) - the seminal monograph analyzing the curvilinear pattern, the role of gender in innovation, social stratification, and the spread of linguistic change across communities.

Archival Socio-Historical Linguistics: Historical Sociolinguistics: Language Change in Tudor and Stuart England (Terttu Nevalainen and Helena Raumolin-Brunberg) - a landmark text establishing how to apply variationist sociolinguistic methods to historical letter collections and textual corpora across social strata.

Digital Methods in Historical Sociolinguistics: Historical Sociolinguistics (Tanja Säily, DARIAH-Campus) - an open-access video masterclass demonstrating how to combine historical corpora, social metadata, and computational tools to analyze language variation in the past.

Empirical Models of Variation and Change: Language Variation and Change (Prof. Edward Flemming, MIT OpenCourseWare) - an advanced university course with lecture notes and problem sets covering phonetic variation, phonological change, and quantitative models of language change in progress.

Etymology, Epigraphy, and Philological Methods

The methodological tools required to trace lexical histories, decipher ancient scripts, and critically evaluate historical textual transmission.

Principles of Etymological Research: The Oxford Guide to Etymology (Philip Durkin) - written by the chief etymologist of the Oxford English Dictionary, this is the authoritative practical guide to evaluating cognates, loanword chronologies, and semantic development.

Decipherment and Epigraphic Systems: The Story of Decipherment: From Egyptian Hieroglyphs to Maya Script (Maurice Pope) - an essential examination of the intellectual history, linguistic principles, and structural methods behind the decipherment of major ancient writing systems.

Textual Criticism and the Philological Method: Scholarly Editing: An Introduction to Textual Criticism (University of Bologna / BOOK Open Knowledge) - an open-access MOOC teaching core philological principles, including manuscript transmission, copying error taxonomy, stemma reconstruction, and critical apparatus preparation.

Manuscript Transmission and Textual History: Scribes and Scholars: A Guide to the Transmission of Greek and Latin Literature, 4th edition (L.D. Reynolds and N.G. Wilson) - a seminal text tracing how handwritten texts survived from antiquity and how textual corruption is identified and rectified to reconstruct earlier linguistic stages.
