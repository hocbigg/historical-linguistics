# Curriculum

## Introduction to Linguistics

A prerequisite subject covering the basic descriptive framework of linguistics — phonetics, phonology, morphology, syntax, and semantics — that every historical linguist needs before studying language change.

[Essentials of Linguistics, 2nd edition (Anderson et al., eCampusOntario)](https://ecampusontario.pressbooks.pub/essentialsoflinguistics2) - a free, openly licensed introductory textbook covering all core areas of linguistics, including a dedicated chapter on historical linguistics; work through the phonetics, phonology, and morphology chapters before moving on.

[MIT OCW 24.900 Introduction to Linguistics (Prof. Norvin Richards)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/24-900-introduction-to-linguistics-spring-2022) - a complete university course with lecture videos, notes, and problem sets; watch the phonetics and phonology lectures (Lectures 5–10) for a structured walkthrough of the concepts you will need.

## Phonetics and Phonology

The study of speech sounds and sound systems, which is essential for understanding how and why sounds change over time.

[Essentials of Linguistics, 2nd edition — Chapter 3: Phonetics and Chapter 4: Phonology](https://ecampusontario.pressbooks.pub/essentialsoflinguistics2) - use these chapters for a compact introduction to articulatory phonetics, the IPA, phonemes, and allophones.

[MIT 24.900 — Lecture 5: Phonetics Part 1 through Lecture 10: Phonology Part 3](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLUl4u3cNGP63BZGNOqrF2qf_yxOjuG35j) - watch these specific lectures from the MIT series for a more detailed, instructor-led treatment; they pair well with the textbook chapters above.

## Sound Change

The core mechanisms by which pronunciation shifts across generations — the foundation of all historical phonology.

[NativLang — Intro to Historical Linguistics: How Languages Change (YouTube playlist)](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLD11358A8871F8587) - a four-lesson animated series that introduces sound change, cognates, and language relationships in an accessible way; start here for a gentle on-ramp before tackling a textbook.

[Trask's Historical Linguistics, 4th edition (R.L. Trask, revised by Robert McColl Millar)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781138954366) - a widely used textbook that covers all types of language change with examples from many language families; the chapters on sound change are the essential reading here.

## The Comparative Method and Linguistic Reconstruction

The core methodology of historical linguistics: how linguists compare related languages to reconstruct unattested ancestral forms.

[The Comparative Method (Robert L. Rankin, UC Berkeley)](https://lx.berkeley.edu/sites/default/files/rankin_comparative_method.pdf) - a clear, authoritative explanation of the comparative method written by a leading practitioner; read this after you have grasped the basics of sound change.

[NativLang — Comparative Method and Language Family Trees (lesson 3) and Reconstruction of Lost Proto-Languages (lesson 4)](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLD11358A8871F8587) - the last two videos in the NativLang series walk through the comparative method with worked examples; a useful visual supplement to Rankin's chapter.

## Language Families and Classification

How languages are grouped into families and subfamilies, and how genetic relationships are distinguished from borrowing and chance resemblance.

[Campbell's Historical Linguistics: An Introduction, 4th edition (Lyle Campbell)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781474463126) - a comprehensive and highly respected textbook that covers language classification, subgrouping, and the distinction between genetic and areal relationships; use the relevant chapters as your primary reference for this topic.

[Essentials of Linguistics, 2nd edition — Chapter 14: Historical Linguistics](https://ecampusontario.pressbooks.pub/essentialsoflinguistics2) - a shorter, freely available overview that covers language change and family relationships; useful as a quick supplement or refresher alongside Campbell.

## Morphological and Syntactic Change

How grammatical structures — word forms, inflections, and sentence patterns — evolve over time, including analogy and grammaticalization.

[Trask's Historical Linguistics, 4th edition](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781138954366) - the chapters on morphological and syntactic change provide the standard treatment of analogy, reanalysis, and grammaticalization.

[Campbell's Historical Linguistics: An Introduction, 4th edition](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781474463126) - Campbell's coverage of morphological change complements Trask's; either book works well on its own, but reading both gives you two perspectives on the same phenomena.

## Semantic and Lexical Change

How word meanings shift over time and how vocabulary is gained, lost, and replaced.

[Trask's Historical Linguistics, 4th edition](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781138954366) - the chapter on semantic change covers broadening, narrowing, amelioration, pejoration, and metaphorical extension with cross-linguistic examples.

[MIT 24.900 — Lecture 23: Historical Linguistics](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5itfLAwQol8) - this single lecture from the MIT series gives a concise overview of semantic drift and other types of change; useful as a summary review after the textbook reading.

## Case Study: Proto-Indo-European

A guided application of everything covered so far, using the best-documented language family to see reconstruction in practice.

[Introduction to Comparative Indo-European Linguistics (Tijmen Pronk, Leiden University / Coursera)](https://www.coursera.org/learn/comparative-indo-european-linguistics) - an eight-module course covering the Indo-European family tree, reconstruction methods, and the oldest attested IE languages; audit mode gives you free access to the content.

[The Horse, the Wheel, and Language (David W. Anthony, Internet Archive)](https://archive.org/details/horsewheelandlanguage) - a landmark book that connects the reconstructed Proto-Indo-European language to its archaeological context on the Eurasian steppe; readable and engaging, it shows how linguistic reconstruction intersects with history and archaeology.
